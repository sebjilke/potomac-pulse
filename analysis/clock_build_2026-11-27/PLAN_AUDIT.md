# Audit of the clock build plan (PLAN_v1.md)

2026-11-27. Independent auditor (Opus). Read-only. I did not run the replay tool and computed no error of any estimate against any truth. The counts come from `onset_correction_2026-11-25/replay_L/shadow_L.csv`, `learn_L.csv`, `counts_L.txt` and `clock_study_2026-11-27/py/units.csv`, `clock_grid.csv`, `cells.csv`. The script is in the scratchpad (`clock_build_audit/counts.py`). Test hours means rows with an estimate, `predTs` from 2020-01-01 to 2026-11-09: 60,120 hours.

The change itself is sound and follows from the study. The replay, as designed, cannot do what its verdict rule asks of it: four of its six conditions are settled by construction. Two definitions it depends on do not exist for a fifth of the hours. Fix those before a specification is written.

## Findings

### F1. BLOCKER. Section 2.2 and 2.5: conditions 1, 2, 3 and 5 are decided in NEW's favour by construction, and "holds on all three truths" protects nothing in the NEW against OLD comparison
Evidence. OLD's logged clock (`travelGFtoLF`) is longer than the proposed clock on all but 109 of 59,746 test hours. It is more than 2 h longer on 42,849 (72%), more than 4 h on 23,710 (40%) and more than 6 h on 10,130 (17%). On rising-limb hours it is more than 2 h longer on 2,060 of 2,975. The study puts OLD at 1.7 to 1.9 times the long end (`cells.csv`). So the long end, the central value and the short end all lie on NEW's side of OLD's clock on almost every hour. NEW learns against LF at `ceilHour(t + clock) - 11 min` (tool lines 469, 470, 716, 767), which is within an hour of the common central truth. On a rise OLD's bins learn residuals against a later and higher LF, so OLD's corrections push the shown value up. Against any earlier truth OLD then reads high, so `d` comes out negative. At low water the clocks barely matter, so `d` is near 0 and condition 3 passes. A failure of 1, 2, 3 or 5 would mean a bug or a learning pathology, not a finding. Condition 4's second half, on OLD's own truth, leans the other way (F7). That makes it the only condition that can fail by construction, and it is the one that carries DO NOT SHIP.
Change. (a) Section 0 and 2: say plainly that the replay cannot show that the shown number gets better. It can show how far the number moves and whether learning stays healthy. The study is the evidence for the clock. (b) Rename 1, 2, 3 and 5 as consistency checks: "expected to hold by construction; a failure stops the work for diagnosis". (c) Report the full 2 by 2 table as description: NEW and OLD, each on its own truth and on the other's truth, plus both on the three common truths. (d) Base SHIP on health conditions that can fail: condition 6 as redefined in F6; missed-window drops, retries, hard skips and blocked hours of NEW not above OLD's by more than a fixed margin (from `counts_*.txt`); the movement of the shown number at high water (F7). Then let the owner decide on the description.

### F2. BLOCKER. Section 2.2: the long-end and short-end truths are not defined for 19% of the test hours or between grid points
Evidence. `clock_grid.csv` gives `long_end` and `short_end` only at seven flows from 1,500 to 15,000 cfs; the rows for 25,000 and 50,000 are `none`. Test hours above 15,000 cfs: 7,597. Below 1,500 cfs: 3,815 (608 below the 1,000 cfs floor). The interval bounds `lo` and `hi` come from a bootstrap at each grid flow, not from a formula. Every condition must hold "on the central truth and on both ends", so conditions 1 and 4 cannot be evaluated as written.
Change. Define both ends as continuous functions of Q. For example: long(Q) = 0.37 x central lag(Q) x r_hi(Q), short(Q) = 0.4 x 0.34 x central lag(Q) x r_lo(Q), with r = hi/L and lo/L interpolated linearly in log Q between grid flows and held at the end values outside 1,500 to 15,000. Or take them from the slope bounds in `fits.csv` (`B_lo`, `B_hi`). Say which, and that outside the grid the ends are extrapolated.

### F3. MAJOR. Section 0 and 1: "one thing changes" is not true. The due time feeds more than the bins
Evidence (all in `scheduled-update.js` unless noted).
- How often a row is scored: the cycle is about `ceil(T)` ticks (`storePrediction` `:2032-2034`, `isExistingPredictionReplaceable` `shared/model.js:886-889`). In the replay OLD already gets 23,688 validations in about 5,458 days, 4.3 a day, median gap 5 h. NEW roughly doubles that.
- Stage bins (`:1520-1590`) learn `rawFinalStage` against the LF stage at the new time.
- EF correlation points (`:1642-1720`) pair the EF stage at estimate time with LF at the due time. They are shown only (R squared in `learning-ui.js:338`; `edwardsFerryData.correlation` is loaded and never read in the estimate).
- `shadow_leaderboard` (`:1922-1952`) and `soft_outlier_event` rows (z against bins that now learn on another truth).
- Headline accuracy: `gf_metadata.sumAbsErrorPercent / validValidations` (`:1777-1782`) is lifetime since the last reset. For months it will mix old-clock and new-clock scores. No action resets only the counters: `resetGFLearning` also wipes the bins.
- Little Falls notice: the R2 rule (latch at -15%, clear above -7.5%, 12 h staleness, `shared/model.js:271-305`) was calibrated on old-clock residuals (v37.15 backtest). With an earlier truth on rises it will latch less often, and with more pairs it will lapse less often. Its confidence notch changes with it.
- Empirical 90% band (`EMPIRICAL_CI_90`, v36.1): it was derived from corrected residuals on the old clock, and its stated meaning is "Little Falls one GF to LF travel time ahead" (`tech-appendix.md:382`, `:409`). After the change it no longer matches what it claims.
- The 7-day chart gets about twice as many points; its summary line "over N h" changes.
- `validation_log.dueAt` (`:2901`) and `tick_log.due` (`:2869`) change meaning without a marker (F9).
Change. List each item in section 1 with a decision. Owner choices: reset the accuracy counters alone (needs a new counters-only admin action) or keep the mix and say so on the page; rebuild the band later (TODO with a trigger). In the replay description, add the notice's duty and number of episodes per run, by running `updateLfResidualState` over each run's scored estimates on its own scoring, and q05 and q95 of the corrected residual per cell under NEW beside the current table.

### F4. MAJOR. Section 2.3: "rising" by rise windows leaves out most hours on which Little Falls is rising, and the groups are not fully defined
Evidence. Test hours inside a rising limb (`start_key` to `peak_key`) of an event or small onset: 2,975 (before 2023-06-01: 1,731; from then: 1,244). With the bumps: 4,488. Test hours on which Little Falls rises by 10% or more within the next 6 h (`lfCap` at t+6 against t, structure only): 4,514. Of these, 2,787 (62%) lie outside every rise limb, bumps included, and 1,932 of those are at 3,000 cfs or more. 789 of them fall in the "low water" group. That is where the clock matters most. Undefined: whether "rise window" means the analysis window `[a, b]` or the limb `[start_key, peak_key]` (low water is 16,140 or 16,682 hours depending on which); whether bumps count; whether units that failed the 0.6 gate or the creek cut count (`units.csv` holds all 225).
Change. Fix the three definitions. Add the group "Little Falls rising" = `LF(t+6h) >= 1.10 x LF(t)` from the hourly record. It uses no model output, and F1 of the study audit targeted the state rule of the clock, not an evaluation group. Exclude those hours from "low water". Report group counts per half before the run.

### F5. MAJOR. Section 1 and the choices: the relation is used outside the flow range of its units, against study audit F12
Evidence. Study audit F12, accepted: "The relation is not used outside the flow range of its units; below that range the low-water relation applies." The fit spans 1,400 to 20,000 cfs (`fits.csv`). The plan extrapolates above 20,000 (4,480 test hours) and between 1,400 and the 1,000 floor. Below 1,500 the low-water phase gives 19.6 h x 0.36 = 7.1 h against 5.5 to 6.5 h from the relation (3,815 test hours); at 2,000 to 3,000 cfs it gives 15.0 x 0.36 = 5.4 h against 4.5 to 4.9 h. Above 12,000 the four unjudged rises have a median of 7.0 h, above the fit (6.4 h at 13,750 cfs), which hints that the lag flattens, not that it keeps falling. Risk above 20,000 is small: NEW minus OLD is at most about 0.85 h there, NEW is longer than OLD on only 103 of those hours, and both land on the same hourly tick on 1,440 of 4,480.
Change. Owner choice, recorded either way. (a) Follow F12: hold Q inside 1,400 to 20,000 (the clock stays at 2.0 h above 20,000), and below 1,500 use the low-water phase values by flow class. This rule uses flow only, so it does not bring back the state problem of study audit F1. (b) Keep the plan's relation and record the rejection of F12 with a reason. In either case add a fourth replay truth at low water from the low-water phases, as description.

### F6. MAJOR. Section 2.5, condition 6: the measure tends to pass as the scoring cadence goes up, and it misses what the user sees
Evidence. With roughly twice as many updates, consecutive steps of the EMA (alpha 0.3) are closer in time and their residuals more alike, so the median step per scored estimate shrinks even if the correction moves more per day. Measurable as written: `learn_X.csv` (`predTs`, `outcome == learned`) joined by `predTs` to `shadow_X.csv` (`correctionApplied`, `predictedCFS`). A per-hour median would be useless: most hours have no learning event, so the median change is about 0 and the ratio is undefined.
Change. Measure per hour from `shadow_X.csv`: |change of `correctionApplied`| / `predictedCFS`, the 95th and 99th percentiles, the number of hours with a jump of 5% or more of the estimate, and the movement over 24 h. Set the 1.5 limit on the 99th percentile and on the jump count, fixed now. Keep the plan's per-step median as description.

### F7. MAJOR. Section 2.5, condition 4: mislabelled and, on OLD's truth, biased toward OLD
Evidence. "12,000 cfs or more" holds 11,751 test hours (first half 6,594, second 5,157). 7,271 of them are at 12,000 to 20,000, inside the fitted range (not extrapolated); 4,480 are at 20,000 or more; 720 are rising-limb hours. The ends of the range do not exist there (F2). "OLD's own truth" can only mean `shadow_L.csv` `truthFlow` at the same `predTs` (NEW's `predictedCFS` against it). The plan does not say so, and section 2.2 says the replay's own scoring is "not used", which contradicts condition 4. On OLD's truth, OLD has the advantage that NEW has on the common truth.
Change. Split 12,000 to 20,000 and 20,000 and above. Name the file and column for OLD's truth. Make the OLD-truth half description (how far NEW's corrections move from OLD's), not a DO NOT SHIP trigger. Keep a bound on how far the shown number moves at 20,000 and above (for example the median and 95th percentile of |NEW - OLD| / OLD).

### F10. MINOR. Q is well defined, with one note

## Answers to the nine questions

1. **Is the one-line change the whole change?** For the estimate before the correction, yes: the model call does not read the due time. For what is learned and shown, no (F3). The due time also sets which LF reading is the truth, so it moves the residual, the anomaly checks 1, 3, 4 and 5 (they take the truth and the EF stage and temperature at validation time), the bins, the stage bins, the EF correlation points, the shadow leaderboard, the headline accuracy, the 7-day history, the LF notice and its confidence notch, and `validation_log`. It also sets how soon the single slot frees, which doubles the scoring cadence. The 2.5 h window and the 48 h stale rule are unchanged in code and in effect. The browser is not a second source of the due time. It recomputes its own GF to LF time for the header, panel and countdown (`great-falls.js:423`), and those must switch to the new function of the browser's LF flow.
2. **Central value, one relation, extrapolation?** The central value is defensible: the four Difficult Run pulses sit at the split, and the long end is only about 23% longer. Using it costs about 1 h if the truth is at the long end, against OLD's 4 to 5 h. One relation for all states is right in real time, because a state rule misassigns hours (study audit F1). Flow-only exceptions do not have that problem, and study audit F12 asks for them: the low-water value below 1,500 cfs (7.1 against 5.5 to 6.5 h) and no extrapolation above 20,000 (F5). The extrapolation above 20,000 risks little: at most about 0.85 h against OLD, and both land on the same hourly tick a third of the time. Owner choice.
3. **What is decided by construction?** NEW cannot fail 1, 2, 3 or 5 unless the learning breaks (F1). The OLD-truth half of 4 leans to OLD and is the only condition NEW can fail by construction (F7). Condition 6 as written leans toward passing (F6). "Holds on all three truths" is no protection for NEW against OLD, because all three truths lie on NEW's side of OLD's clock on all but 109 of 59,746 hours. It is real protection only for comparisons within run NEW (the cap, the yardsticks), where the three truths do bracket NEW's clock. No truth is fair to both runs, because nothing measures GF. The fair design is the 2 by 2 (each run on each run's truth) plus the common truths, all as description. Decide on the health conditions.
4. **The group "rising" and the thresholds?** Sound against study audit F1 (windows, not a 2% rule) and F10 (three truths, for within-NEW readings). But it holds only 2,975 test hours (1,731 and 1,244 by half; 4,488 with bumps) and misses 62% of the hours on which LF rises 10% or more within 6 h (F4). Low water: 16,140 hours. High water: 11,751 (4,480 at 20,000 or more). With the rising interval taken over calendar months, the +0.05 and +0.10 point limits are tight but workable. Since conditions 1 to 3 pass by construction, the thresholds matter less than the reframing.
5. **Condition 6?** Measurable from `learn_X.csv` joined to `shadow_X.csv` by `predTs`, but biased toward passing when the cadence doubles. Use per-hour percentiles and a jump count, with the 1.5 limit on those (F6).
6. **Forecast offsets left out?** Right in scope, since they change the cards and have their own scoring. The interim is visible in two places: the header pair (PoR to LF 25.8 h against PoR to GF 19.4 plus GF to LF 3.4 at 5,000 cfs) and the How It Works text. The forecast cards do not show their offset. Document it, and add a TODO with a trigger (F15).
7. **Stored state at release?** Safe. The row in flight blocks the new clock for at most about 17 h (replay maximum 16.95 h). If it is more than 12 h, the LF notice may lapse once, as it can today. A missed row is replaceable after due plus 2.5 h. No reset of the bins is needed. The accuracy counters are the open question (F3).
8. **Missing test or parity rule?** The constants belong in `constants.js` under the parity rule. The four tests that must change on purpose need naming. Add the exact due-time test, the non-convergence test, a whole-object equality test and the edge inputs (F8). Leaving `travelTimeGFtoLF` untouched avoids silent changes in the estimate. It does not avoid the two-clock ambiguity in the row and the logs (F9), or the silent changes listed in F3.
9. **The case against, and a smaller first step:** see below.

## The strongest case against building it now

The replay cannot show that the change is right or that it helps. Every condition that could show a gain is settled by the clock it is scored on, and the clock rests on an untested split by distance (36%) and four Difficult Run pulses. So the decision is really "trust the study". The replay only adds a check that nothing breaks, and it is planned as about ten agents and several hours for conditions whose answer is known. The change also lowers the shown number on rising water, which is the dangerous side if the split is wrong (F17). And it does nothing for the creek-led rise that started this work.

Against that: the study's verdict does not depend on the split being exact. OLD is 1.7 to 1.9 times even the long end, and a scoring clock 4 to 5 h too long at 3,000 to 5,000 cfs is a known bias in every bin.

Smaller first step I recommend: (1) run the two replays now (about 5 s each) and report the description alone: the 2 by 2 table, how far the shown number moves by group, condition 6 as redefined, the notice and the band, the health counters. Put the owner's decision on that, with the consistency checks as stop rules. (2) If the owner wants live evidence, ship first a MINOR log-only release: compute `clock(Q)`, write it to `tick_log` and the pending row, and add to each scored row the LF reading at the new due time, never learned from. Two weeks of that give the live cadence and the notice behaviour without touching the bins. It cannot say which clock is right either, so it is optional.

Verdict: PROCEED AFTER CHANGES. F1 and F2 must be fixed before a specification. F3 to F9 should be. F5 and the accuracy counters (F3) are owner choices.
