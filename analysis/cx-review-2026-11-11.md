# Part 1. Visitor journey and comprehension

POTOMAC PULSE: CUSTOMER EXPERIENCE REVIEW (visitor journey and comprehension)
Read-only. I changed no files. Everything below is from source (index.html, src/ui/*.js, main.css, init.js, fetch.js, CLAUDE.md, README). I fetched only the live `/` HTML. Its title matches the source; no other check was needed. I have not seen a rendered page. Where a point depends on rendering, it is marked INFERRED.

## Overall impression
The page is honest about its limits, but it says so in the wrong places and at the wrong size. A paddler's first screen shows the real Little Falls gauge first (labelled "Target gauge"), then a "PoR → LF" travel-time line they can't decode. The Great Falls estimate comes after that. The card gives its own warnings, confidence label and data-source line in 8.8 to 9.6 px text. Several things a paddler needs are missing from the card or hidden:
- the age of the data (only in a header pill that may not fit at 360 px);
- the 90% range (hidden inside a collapsed panel);
- a plain statement that a low number on a rising river can be wrong, which exists only in prose on the How It Works tab.

Nothing stops the "ESTIMATED NOW" number from being shown when its inputs are hours old. Up to three amber and purple notices can stack in the same tiny type, so they read as noise. The Learning tab is a locked PIN screen that most visitors can't use. There is no way to report a problem except a GitHub link. The jokes (Toby, Tim, "Click me!") sit next to the only safety statement.

## Findings, ranked by impact on a paddler

**1. The estimate still says "ESTIMATED NOW" when its inputs are old, and the card shows no age.** OBSERVED IN CODE (the stale-input suppression check is by grep, not a full read).
- Visitor sees: a bold cfs number in a card labelled "ESTIMATED NOW". The only age signal is a pill in the header (`#lastUpdate`, `data-freshness.js` describeFreshness). The estimate path has no staleness gate: `great-falls-ui.js` renders whatever `estimateGreatFalls()` returns, and `great-falls.js` has no age check.
- Why it matters: a 7-hour-old river on a rising day looks like current data. The header pill turns red at 6 h, but it sits away from the number.
- Change: put the age inside the card, directly under the number: "Based on gauge readings from 14:40 (42 min ago)". Past about 3 h, change the label to "ESTIMATE FROM OLD READINGS" and grey the number. Past about 6 h, show "--" with "Data too old to estimate" (no number).
- Effort: small for the age line, medium for the gate.

**2. Safety-critical text is tiny.** OBSERVED IN CODE.
- Sizes (index.html): confidence `0.55rem` (8.8 px), "cfs (LF scale)" `0.55rem`, data source `0.55rem`, cap note and both advisories `0.6rem`, "*Experimental" `0.6rem`, creek disclaimer "Thresholds are approximate" `0.55rem`. The browser's minimum-font setting is not applied to rem sizes, so these render at 9 to 10 px on a phone.
- Why it matters: this is the text that tells you not to trust the number. It is also the first thing lost with glare or cold hands.
- Change: floor at 0.75rem (12 px) for any warning, confidence or unit text. Make the confidence label at least 0.85rem and put it on the same line as the number (see 4).
- Effort: small.

**3. The cap note and the two advisories stack into a wall of small amber text, and the first one is the most alarming.** OBSERVED IN CODE.
- Titles: "⚠️ Cross-check gauge disagrees — treat this estimate with extra caution." and "⚠️ Recent estimates ran low — treat this one with extra caution." Both use the same amber box, so the second looks like a repeat of the first. The confidence label then also gets a "⚠" and loses up to two notches (`great-falls-ui.js` 205–217). The visitor cannot tell what to do.
- The bodies are good ("If the river looks higher than the number, believe the river."). Only the titles and the stacking are the problem.
- Change:
  - Merge into one box, "Caution: estimate may be too low", with one bullet per active reason.
  - Put "If the river looks higher than the number, believe the river." on its own bold line at the end.
  - Show the confidence downgrade as "LOW (lowered by 2 warnings)" so the label matches the boxes.
  - Replace the second title with "The estimate has been running low compared with Little Falls."
- Effort: medium.

**4. The confidence label is easy to over-trust, and its caveat is far from it.** OBSERVED IN CODE (the label) and in `index.html` 357 (the caveat).
- Visitor sees: "Confidence: HIGH" in small purple or green text. The caveat that "a few High readings were off by more than 25%" and that it "is not a measured error rate" is in the How It Works tab, a different tab. A High reading next to a falling or capped number reads as "safe to trust".
- Change: show the label as a range-based plain statement in the card: "Typical miss about 9%. Could be 25% or more off." Keep High/Medium/Low as a second line if wanted. Add a "?" that jumps to the How It Works confidence entry.
- Effort: small.

**5. Direction of error is not stated next to the number.** OBSERVED IN CODE and INFERRED.
- The estimate is capped at 120% of the last Little Falls reading and is blind to local rain. The dangerous miss is "reads low while the river rises". The cap note says this ("The real flow may be above the number shown, especially on a rising river"), but only when the cap is active, at 0.6rem. A rising trend shows as a green ▲ in `#gf-trend` with no warning attached.
- Change: when the state is RISING, add a fixed line under the trend: "Rising rivers can read low here. Check the river before you launch." Do not condition it on the cap.
- Effort: small.

**6. The 90% range never appears on the card.** OBSERVED IN CODE.
- `#gf-ci-range` is `display:none`. Nothing sets it visible (the only references hide it, `great-falls-ui.js` 87, 119, 170). The range lives only in the collapsed "📥 Estimation Inputs ▸" panel as "90% CI". The README and docs promise a band, so the visitor can't find it.
- Change: show it under the big number: "Likely range 9,000 – 12,000 cfs" (plain wording, not "90% CI"). Define it once: "9 times out of 10 the real flow is inside this range." Keep the "(around the capped value)" suffix.
- Effort: small. The data is already computed.

**7. First 10 seconds: jargon and order.** OBSERVED IN CODE.
- Visitor sees, top to bottom: "🎯 Little Falls · 11,560 sq mi • Target gauge", cfs and ft, then "PoR → LF: --" (aria says "travel time from Point of Rocks to Little Falls"), then the tab bar, then "ESTIMATED CONDITIONS AT Great Falls". "Target gauge", "sq mi" and "PoR → LF" mean nothing to a visitor. The data-source line is worse: "83% PoR (19h ago) [Δ-corrected 12%] + 17% EF → LF in ~6.5 hrs".
- Why it matters: the first number they see is the Little Falls reading, not the Great Falls estimate, and the unit "ft (LF scale)" is a gauge height that is not Great Falls stage. A paddler who knows Great Falls by its own levels can misread it.
- Change:
  - Put the Great Falls estimate card at the top; move Little Falls below it as "Measured at Little Falls (downstream)".
  - Replace "Target gauge" with "Real gauge, 9 miles downstream" (check the distance).
  - Replace "PoR → LF" with "Water takes about 26 h from Point of Rocks to Little Falls".
  - Replace "ft (LF scale)" with "ft on the Little Falls gauge scale. Not the Great Falls level."
  - Spell out jargon in the source line: "Mostly from the Point of Rocks gauge 19 hours ago, plus Edwards Ferry".
- Effort: medium. The first step is small.

**8. The forecast looks more precise than it is.** OBSERVED IN CODE.
- Forecast cards show stage to two decimals (`p.stage.toFixed(2) ft`) and cfs to the unit. The "*Experimental — collecting data to validate accuracy" line is 0.6rem. The "FORECAST RELIABILITY" scale (6 hr Good, 12 hr Fair, 24 hr Rough, 48 hr Directional) lives only in the How It Works tab. The cards are labelled "NWS" or "NWS+EF" (`sourceLabel`), which means nothing to visitors. CLAUDE.md states the forecast has "no independent content beyond a time-shift" of the NWS forecast. The page says this only in How It Works.
- Change: round cards to 0.1 ft and to the nearest 100 cfs. Add the reliability words to each card ("6 h: good", "48 h: direction only"). Replace "NWS" with "National Weather Service forecast, shifted". Move the "experimental" note to normal size and wording: "Experimental forecast. Check NWS river forecasts."
- Effort: small to medium.

**9. The mobile header probably overflows at 360 px.** INFERRED (not rendered).
- Header is `display:flex; justify-content:space-between` with no wrap and no `min-width:0`. It holds the title plus a 44 px ⓘ button, a dot, the reading-age pill (e.g. "Readings 7h old, not refreshed since 14:20, Little Falls missing" can be about 60 characters), and two 40 px buttons. The pill alone is wider than the space left.
- Why it matters: the age signal is the one honest freshness cue, and it is the part that gets squeezed or pushed off.
- Change: on ≤420 px, wrap the header to two rows: title and buttons on row 1, full-width age pill on row 2. Shorten the pill text for the stale case ("Data 7 h old").
- Effort: small.

**10. The tab bar will clip labels at 360 px, and the inner scroll area fights the page scroll.** INFERRED from CSS.
- At ≤420 px `.tab` is 0.65rem with `white-space:nowrap; overflow:hidden; text-overflow:ellipsis` across five tabs. "📖 How It Works" and "🌊 Great Falls" will be ellipsised. The tab bar sits at the top of the sidebar (sticky), which is out of thumb reach.
- The Great Falls and How It Works panels also have inline `max-height:calc(100vh - 280px); overflow-y:auto` (`index.html` 107, 245). That creates a scroller inside the page's single scroll container (the #20 fix removed `.tab-content`'s own scroll but not this one). On a phone, with the 100vh unit, this gives two nested scrollers and a footer that eats space.
- Change: remove the inline max-heights on mobile; use `dvh`. Shorten labels ("Falls", "Creeks", "Gauges", "Guide"). Consider a bottom tab bar.
- Effort: small to medium.

**11. The Learning tab is a dead end for visitors and its name promises something it doesn't give.** OBSERVED IN CODE.
- Tab label "🔒 Learning" opens "🔒 Protected Section — Enter PIN" (`index.html` 454–465). The brief says the app shows "how the model learns". Ordinary visitors get a padlock. The 7-day accuracy chart, which the How It Works text refers to ("they still appear on the accuracy chart as hollow rings"), is inside the locked part, so that sentence points at something they can't see.
- Why it matters: it wastes a tab in a cramped bar, and it makes the page look half-finished or secret. It also hides accuracy evidence that would help build trust.
- Change: remove the tab from public view (keep it behind a URL such as `#admin`). Publish a small read-only "How accurate has it been?" panel (last 7 days: typical miss, worst miss) in How It Works.
- Effort: small to hide, medium to publish.

**12. How It Works: findable, but dense and jargon-heavy.** OBSERVED IN CODE.
- The tab is easy to find. The top paragraph is good ("There is no gauge at Great Falls…"). The "Self-correcting" block (`index.html` 348) is one long paragraph with "quarantined", "clamp", "hollow rings", "accuracy average", "v39.0", "correction factors". The "Be honest about what this is" paragraph mentions "v37.16" and "persistence". The paddler-relevant facts are buried in these.
- The "This is an estimate, not a measurement" box is good, but it sits at the bottom after the diagram and the long text.
- Change: move the safety box and a 3-line "What this is / what it is not" to the top of the tab. Move the "Self-correcting" and "Be honest" detail into a collapsed "Technical detail" section. Delete version numbers from visitor-facing prose.
- Effort: small to medium.

**13. Safety statement and tone.** OBSERVED IN CODE.
- The only global safety line is the footer's "Not for navigation or life-safety decisions", in 0.7rem muted grey at the very bottom, with a joke tooltip ("Toby Woby is working on it..."; hover turns it into that joke, double-click into "STILL working on it"). On a phone it is below a nested scroller so it is rarely seen. The About popover (ⓘ) is mostly jokes ("Tim is already wet").
- The line is also worded for aviation or marine navigation, not for paddlers. It doesn't say what to do.

**14. Returning visitor, offline and stale tab.** OBSERVED IN CODE (behaviour) and INFERRED (experience).

Bad: nothing says "changed since you last looked" (no "up 1,200 cfs since 2 h ago" on the card). The sparkline history gives some of this, but only if the visitor reads the graph. The error banner for a failed refresh vanishes after 10 s while the data stays old, and the only remaining signal is the small pill. "Showing cached data" doesn't say how old. The offline bar is orange text on dark, "Offline — showing last-known data", with no age.

- "cfs (LF scale)" / "ft (LF scale)" -> "cfs" / "ft on the Little Falls gauge scale".

## Done well, don't change
- The reading-age label measures the readings, not the fetch time, and escalates in colour (`data-freshness.js`). The "Saved copy:" wording and the missing-gauge suffix are clear.
- The advisory bodies end with "If the river looks higher than the number, believe the river." Keep that sentence and make it more prominent.
- Creek cards avoid "Running!" claims, state the multiple of the minimum, say "Potomac Pulse has no high-water level for this creek", and show old readings as history ("The current level is unknown."). The banner never says "Nothing running" while data is missing.
- The How It Works intro and the "This is an estimate, not a measurement" box are honest and well worded. The October 2026 miss figures (median 9%, some High readings off by 25%+) are exactly the kind of honesty that builds trust. They are just in the wrong place.
- Admin and diagnostic material is mostly separated (PIN-gated), and the skip link, ARIA roles, 44 px tap targets on buttons and tabs, and offline bar are in place.
- The "Typical (~5k cfs)" row is highlighted in the travel-time table, which is useful context.

## 2. Findings, ranked by impact on a paddler

**F1. A slow (not failed) `/api/sync` freezes the first load. Effort: small. Risk: low. OBSERVED IN CODE.**
- `init()` does `await loadGFLearningData()` before `initMap`, `buildBranches`, `buildCreeks`, `initTabs` and `fetchData` (`src/init.js` ~L59-74). That loader uses a plain `fetch` with no timeout (`src/learning/gf-learning.js:42`). Only a hard error falls through to the catch.
- On poor signal the request can hang for a long time. Meanwhile the tabs don't switch (`initTabs` has not run), the gauge list is empty and USGS isn't requested. The 15-minute cycle has the same flaw: it awaits the learning load before `fetchData` (`src/main.js:57-60`), so one hung call stalls all refreshes.
- The USGS fetch doesn't depend on the learning data, and the GF card already shows a loading state.
- Change: add a timeout of 4-6 s (AbortController). Start `fetchData()` in parallel with the learning load. Run the UI-build calls first. In `main.js`, don't await the learning load before `fetchData`.

**F2. The Great Falls card can sit on "..." / "Loading EF data..." forever, with no failure message. Effort: small-medium. Risk: medium (touches the estimate gate). OBSERVED IN CODE.**
- The card renders only if `gfDataReady && edwardsFerryData.current !== null` (`great-falls-ui.js:78-92`). The Edwards Ferry (EF) fetch has a 5 s timeout and returns silently on failure (`edwards-ferry.js:20-60`). EF state is in memory only, so a returning visit has no EF value either.
- EF has weight about 0% below about 6k cfs, so the estimate is computable without it. The card still never appears.
- The same "..." shows when USGS is down and no cache exists (`data:unavailable` calls `updateUI`). The user sees "Loading..." while the header says "No Data". Not truthful.
- Change: distinguish "loading" from "failed". After the fetch settles, show "Estimate unavailable: no data from USGS. Check gauge page / try again" with a retry button. Decide separately whether to allow a PoR-only estimate when EF is missing. That is a model decision, so it needs the owner.

**F3. A saved or old number still reads "ESTIMATED NOW" with no age on the card. Effort: small. Risk: low. OBSERVED IN CODE.**
- The header label does age correctly (`data-freshness.js`). The card itself has no age or "saved copy" mark.
- The localStorage fallback is returned at any age. `CACHE_MAX_AGE` (6 h) only changes the label to "stale" (`fetch.js` `fetchUSGS`; `constants.js:6`). A 3-day-old cache would still produce an "ESTIMATED NOW" figure.
- The "stale/offline" error banner auto-hides after 10 s (`showErrorBanner`, `fetch.js` ~L600). The remaining signal is a small header label.
- Change: when readings are over 2 h old or the source is saved/stale, replace the label with "OLD ESTIMATE, based on readings from <clock time>" and dim the number. Past roughly 6-12 h of reading age, withhold the number and show the last readings as history. Keep the banner until dismissed.

**F4. The forecast has no age or source freshness. Effort: medium. Risk: low-medium. OBSERVED / INFERRED.**
- NWS forecast calls go through the service worker, which falls back to a cache up to 6 h old after a 4 s timeout (`runtime-caching.mjs`, live `sw.js`). `hoursAhead` is recomputed from "now", so an old forecast is silently shifted and shown as current. No issuance time is read or displayed (grep for `issued` returned nothing).
- When the forecast is missing, the fallback is linear extrapolation. It is labelled only by a small "extrapolated" tag next to each card, while the heading still says "48H FORECAST".
- Change: read the NWS forecast's issue time. Show "NWS forecast issued 14:00" and mark it stale past about 8 h. When extrapolating, say so in the heading.

**F5. USGS data is routed through two public CORS proxies (privacy and integrity). Effort: medium. Risk: medium. OBSERVED IN CODE.**
- When the direct USGS payload looks old, the app asks `api.allorigins.win` and `corsproxy.io` (`fetch.js` `PROXIES_LIST`, ~L400+). A payload that passes only a schema check is accepted and used as the river reading.
- A proxy (or someone who compromises one) could return false flows. Passing the schema check is all that is required.
- Every proxied request also leaks the user's IP and the query to a third party. The `NetworkFirst` SW rule for proxies means a proxy answer can be cached as well.
- This also fires in a harmless case: a slow SW fallback to an old direct payload triggers the proxy chain.
- Change: drop the proxies (`waterservices.usgs.gov` sends CORS headers; the direct call works, per the code comments). If kept, cross-check against the direct payload and cap how much a proxy value may differ. If proxies stay, say so in a privacy line.

**F6. No refresh on wake, tab focus or reconnect. Effort: small. Risk: low. OBSERVED IN CODE.**
- There is no `visibilitychange` or `pageshow` handler (grep returned nothing). `online` and `offline` only redraw the label (`init.js`). Refresh is a 15 min `setInterval` that mobile browsers throttle or freeze.
- After the phone wakes, the user sees the old numbers until they tap ↻ or a timer fires. The label ages, but the numbers do not update.
- Change: on `visibilitychange` (visible), `online` and `pageshow`, call `fetchData()` if the last fetch is over about 5 min old. Existing `isFetching` guards double calls.

**F7. Key text is very small and some of it is low contrast. Effort: small-medium. Risk: low. OBSERVED IN CODE.**
- Safety-relevant lines use 0.5-0.6rem (about 8-10 px): confidence, cap note, advisories, the "Experimental" notice and the creek "scout" warning (`index.html` L99, L128, L135, L138, L159). Units ("cfs (LF scale)") are 0.55rem.
- `--text-muted #64748b` on `#0f172a` is about 3.7:1 and `--text-faint #475569` about 2.3:1 (my calculation from the token values). Both fail WCAG AA for body text.
- There is no dark/light choice, so sun glare on the river is a likely problem (INFERRED).
- No `prefers-reduced-motion` rule (grep returned none) while there are infinite `pulse` and `spin` animations.
- The forecast cards are `div` elements with `onclick` and no role or tabindex, and the SVG graph uses mouse/touch only (`great-falls-ui.js` ~L580; `forecast-graph.js:301-304`). Keyboard users cannot read the graph or cards.
- Tabs, creek cards, the skip link and the tab arrow keys are done correctly.

**F8. The safety disclaimer is easy to miss and partly turned into a joke. Effort: small. Risk: low. OBSERVED IN CODE.**
- The explicit callout ("This is an estimate, not a measurement. Do not use it for life-safety decisions.") sits only on the How It Works tab (`index.html` L409-416). The main tab has only "ESTIMATED NOW" plus "*Experimental" on the forecast.

- Change: put a one-line "Estimate, not a measurement. Check NWS warnings." under the GF number. Move the easter egg off the disclaimer element.

**F9. First-load weight and request count. Effort: medium. Risk: medium. OBSERVED, sizes INFERRED.**
- The main USGS call is `period=P7D` for all gauges with both discharge and stage. The code comments give a 2.9 MB body. It is then `JSON.stringify`-ed whole into localStorage (`saveToCache`). Two bytes per character puts that near or over a typical 5 MB cap (INFERRED, device-dependent). If the write fails, `catch` only logs. The cache then silently never exists, and PoR/GF history writes (same storage) may also fail.
- Only PoR needs about 72 h (history max about 3 days). The 7-day window serves little beyond that.
- Request count on a cold load: roughly 1 HTML (68 KB) + JS 363 KB + CSS 33 KB, up to 5 `/api/sync` reads, 3 USGS, 2 geojson (301 KB NHD + 5 KB) fetched even though the map is hidden (`map.js:55,70`), and NWS: 12 gauges x (up to 4 endpoint tries + 1 category call) = about 24-60 calls every 15 min (`nws.js`). Plus GoatCounter. Rough total 40-80 requests. The UI renders before NWS arrives, which is good.
- Change: request P3D, or stage only where the app uses it. Store only the needed fields in the cache. Lazy-load the geojson when the map is first opened. Fetch NWS only for the 3 gauges the forecast uses (LF, PoR, EF) and load flood categories on demand. The SW already precaches the geojson, so the first visit pays for it twice.

**F10. "Last ran" on creeks is not when the creek last ran. Effort: small. Risk: low. OBSERVED IN CODE.**
- It is stamped with the browser's current time whenever this device sees the creek running (`fetch.js` `fetchCreekData`, `localStorage creek_lastran_*`). A creek that ran yesterday while the app was closed shows "unknown" or a wrong age. Different devices disagree.
- Change: derive it from the 24 h history already fetched ("ran within the last 24 h: yes/no"), or drop the line.

**F11. The uncertainty range is not shown where the number is, and may be mislabelled. Effort: small. Risk: medium. OBSERVED IN CODE.**

Showing it as "90%" next to the number would repeat that error. The TODO item matters more than its priority suggests, because this is the main number.

**F12. Developer and admin material in the public UI. Effort: small. Risk: low. OBSERVED IN CODE.**

A 6-character PIN can be brute-forced offline, and the unlocked panel is only hidden, not absent. TODO says the server PIN gates POST only; the GET admin reads are public. **F14. App updates arrive silently. Effort: small-medium. Risk: low. OBSERVED / INFERRED.**

|---|---|
