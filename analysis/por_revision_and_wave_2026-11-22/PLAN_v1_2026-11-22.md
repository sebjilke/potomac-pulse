# Study plan: the Point of Rocks revision and the daily wave (November 2026)

**Status:** DRAFT v1, written 2026-11-22 (UTC) by the orchestrator. Not yet audited. Not yet confirmed by the owner.
No analysis code has been written. Nothing in the app, the hourly job or the database is changed by this study.

**Owner's request (2026-11-22):** "study the wave, and do the same for the USGS correction. I wanna fully
understand both patterns before discussing remedies!"

## 0. In plain words

Since 14 November the Great Falls estimate has been off by about 14% on average (2.6% in the days before, 7.4%
long-run). A first look by the orchestrator found two patterns:

1. **The revision.** On 13 November USGS rewrote its published flow at Point of Rocks: about 30% more flow at
   the same water level. Point of Rocks is the model's main input.
2. **The daily wave.** The river at Point of Rocks goes up and down by about 0.2 ft every day. The estimate
   follows that wave poorly, and worse since the revision.

This study describes both patterns completely: what exactly happened, how each one travels through every part
of the model, and how the two interact. It is descriptive. It proposes no remedy and changes nothing. Remedies
are discussed with the owner afterwards, on the basis of the report.

## 2. Questions

Tier: **core** is needed to explain November 2026. **extended** places it in a longer history. The owner asked
for a full understanding, so the plan covers both; the extended items can be dropped at confirmation.

### Part R: the USGS revision

- **R1 (core). Anatomy of the revision.** For every Point of Rocks reading for which we hold a copy from before
  the revision, compare the old flow with the flow USGS publishes now at the identical timestamp. Report the
  ratio new/old by water level and by date; say whether the change is best described as a percent change, a
  fixed number of cfs, or a shift of the level-to-flow curve by a fixed number of feet; find the earliest
  reading whose flow changed and the earliest reading stamped 2026-11-13 19:41.
- **R2 (core). Was anything else revised?** The same old-against-current comparison for Little Falls, Monocacy,
  Goose Creek, Seneca Creek, Broad Run (flow) and for the water levels at Point of Rocks, Edwards Ferry and
  Little Falls. Expected: no change. Any change is reported, whatever its size.
- **R3 (core). USGS's own account.** All field measurements at Point of Rocks in the new USGS service: date,
  level, measured flow, control condition, rating of the measurement, method, entry time. How often does USGS
  visit, and how far apart were consecutive measurements at similar levels in earlier years.
- **R4 (core). Passage through the model, hour by hour, 10 to 22 Nov.** For every hourly run: the chain
  Point of Rocks flow -> assumed travel time and time-shifted reading -> sum with tributaries -> rise/fall
  factor ("delta") -> Edwards Ferry blend -> raw estimate -> flow state and bin -> learned correction -> final
  estimate -> 120% cap. Three phases are described separately: before the revision; the transition (the job's
  stored 72-hour history holds old and new numbers together); after it. Then counterfactual replays with the
  production code (section 4): "revision never happened" and "revised numbers from the start".
- **R5 (core). How the learned corrections responded.** Each bin's value before and after every check
  (`validation_log`, `bin_snapshot`), the size of the gap still open, the number of checks each bin received,
  and the rate of closing implied by the learning rule (EMA, alpha 0.3). No forecast of recovery beyond that.
- **R6 (core). Exposure of the other flow ranges.** From R1's ratio by level: for each of the six flow ranges,
  how much the revision changes the Point of Rocks number at typical levels of that range, set against the
  correction each bin currently holds. Descriptive: which bins were learned against numbers that no longer
  exist, and by how much.
- **R7 (extended). How often does this happen?** (a) Two frozen datasets in the repository,
  `hourly_backtest_data_v361.csv` (pulled 2026-06-16) and `hourly_backtest_data_v38.csv` (pulled 2026-07-20),
  overlap almost completely: every hourly value that differs between them is a revision USGS made in those
  five weeks. Count and size by gauge. (b) The same for `v38` against USGS today, for the twelve months before
  the pull, if the hourly-median method can be reproduced exactly through the new service (a fidelity check on
  approved, unchanged months decides; if it fails, (b) is dropped and reported as not done).

### Part W: the daily wave

- **W1 (core). Description.** At Point of Rocks (level and flow), Edwards Ferry (level) and Little Falls (level
  and flow), 15-minute data, 1 September to 22 November 2026: period, size (two measures, section 5), time of
  crest and trough, shape, day of week, and the dates on which it starts, weakens or strengthens.
- **W2 (extended). Origin.** The same description at the gauges upstream (candidates, to be verified against
  USGS site information before use: Potomac at Shepherdstown 01618000, Potomac at Hancock 01613000, Shenandoah
  at Millville 01636500, Potomac at Paw Paw 01610000, and the larger tributaries between them). Which branch
  carries the wave, and when it passes each gauge. The physical cause (for example dam operation) is stated
  only if a public source documents it; otherwise it is reported as unknown.
- **W3 (core). Travel and damping from Point of Rocks to Little Falls.** Delay and loss of size of the wave
  between Point of Rocks, Edwards Ferry and Little Falls. A 24-hour wave fixes the delay only up to whole days,
  so the delay is pinned down three ways that must agree: matching the day-to-day changes in the wave's size
  (for example the weak days of 15 and 16 Nov at Point of Rocks), the intermediate gauge (the two legs must add
  up), and individual crests. The result is set against the travel time the model assumes for the water. It is
  also checked that the measured delay does not change at the revision (the river did not change that day).
- **W4 (core). How the model passes the wave on.** For each component in R4's chain: its daily size and timing,
  before and after the revision, against the truth (Little Falls at each estimate's own due time). Every hourly
  estimate is scored, not only the roughly two a day that the job checks itself. The hourly error is split
  into three parts: a constant offset, a daily cycle (mean by hour of day), and the remainder.
- **W5 (core). Why is the wave tracked worse since the revision?** Five candidate explanations, each with the
  observation that would support or reject it:
  - H1 mistiming: the model derives its travel time from the Point of Rocks flow number, which rose 30% while
    the river did not speed up. Test: 2 x 2 replay (old/new flow numbers x old/new travel time), section 4.
  - H2 the wave itself changed (larger at Point of Rocks, or passed on differently to Little Falls). Test: W1
    and W3 before and after.
  - H3 the rise/fall factor reacts differently to the new numbers. Test: its daily cycle in the replays.
  - H4 switching between corrections: the estimate changes flow state over the day, and the three states'
    corrections now differ by up to about 270 cfs. Test: daily cycle of raw against final.
  - H5 nothing changed: 5 checks before and 15 after, at two times of day, are too few. Test: the hourly
    scoring of W4, with its uncertainty.
- **W6 (core). What the job's own checks see.** The job checks one estimate at a time, about every 13 hours at
  this flow. At which times of day do the checks fall, which part of the daily error cycle do they sample, and
  which phase of the wave feeds each of the three state bins.
- **W7 (extended). Is this a recurring pattern?** (a) 2011 to 2026, hourly flow at Point of Rocks and Little
  Falls (`hourly_backtest_data_v38.csv`): on how many days, at which flows, in which seasons and years is there
  a daily wave, and how large. (b) In the existing replay of the production model over 2011 to 2026
  (`analysis/replay_lag_2026-11-17/`, arm F, hourly shadow estimates): error by size of the wave that day.

the pull, the builder stops and reports, and the owner decides).

## 4. Tools to build, and their gates

1. **Freeze script** (data builder): exports and pulls of section 3, hashes, a manifest.
2. **Tidy tables** (data builder): one long table of readings (gauge, parameter, time, value, source file, pull
   time), one table of hourly runs (flattened `tick_log`), one of checks. Gate: row counts reconcile with the
   raw files; 20 random cells traced back by hand by the tool auditor.
3. **Old curve** `f_old(level)`: the level-to-flow relation USGS used before the revision, from the old pairs
   (job log and October pull). It is a lookup, not a model: USGS computes flow from level deterministically.
   Gate: reproduces every old pair within the rounding USGS applies; reported separately by date, because USGS
   shifts can vary in time; where the old pairs of October and of 10 to 13 Nov disagree at the same level, both
   versions are carried as a sensitivity. Levels below the lowest old pair need extrapolation: reported as such,
   with two extrapolation rules as bounds.
4. **Replay tool**: runs the production prediction code (frozen export of the current commit, imported as the
   October flood audit did: `makeGFPrediction` and the shared helpers; no network, no database) once per hourly
   run with the inputs that run had. **Fidelity gate (must pass before any scenario is run): the replay of the
   as-run inputs reproduces the logged raw estimate of every run within 2 cfs; every miss is explained.**
   Scenarios:
   - S0 as run (the log itself).
   - S-old "revision never happened": Point of Rocks flow = `f_old(level)` at every timestamp.
   - S-new "revised numbers from the start": Point of Rocks flow = USGS's current record at every timestamp.
   - S-new-oldtime and S-old-newtime: the 2 x 2 of W5/H1, by overriding the travel time with the other
     scenario's value. The override is the only change to the production path and is audited as such.
   The scenarios replay the raw estimate. Where a final estimate is shown for a scenario, it uses the bins
   frozen at their last state before the revision and is labelled "approximate"; the study does not replay
   learning under counterfactual inputs (the schedule of checks would itself change; stated as a limit).
5. **Truth series**: Little Falls flow at each estimate's due time, two versions: (T1) interpolated between the
   two neighbouring 15-minute readings; (T2) the reading the job itself would have used at the first run at or
   after the due time. Gate: T2 reproduces `actualCFS` of all 20 logged checks.

An independent auditor (Opus) reviews tools 1 to 5 against this plan before the analysts start.

## 5. Definitions

- Times in UTC. Hour of day is also shown in local time (America/New_York; clocks changed on 2026-11-01).
- Error = (estimate - truth) / truth x 100; positive means the estimate is too high. "Raw" is the estimate
  before the learned correction; "final" is what the app shows.
- Size of the wave, two measures that must tell the same story: (a) half of (max - min) of the series after
  subtracting its centred 24-hour moving average, per 24 hours; (b) amplitude and phase of a 24-hour cosine
  fitted with a straight-line trend over sliding 72-hour windows. Relative size = size / window mean.
- Delay between two gauges: lag of the maximum cross-correlation of the two series after the same 24-hour
  detrending, at 15-minute steps, all local maxima reported; resolved as in W3.
- "Before" = runs up to 2026-11-13 19:00 UTC; "transition" = from 19:41 UTC until the stored 72-hour history
  holds only new numbers (the builder determines the hour from the log); "after" = from then on.
- Flow ranges and states are the production ones (6 ranges x 3 states).

## 6. Expected outcomes, written before any result (they may be wrong)

- R1: new/old about 1.25 to 1.32 at levels of 0.9 to 1.3 ft in November; a smaller ratio at flood levels;
  readings restamped back to late August, but whether flows changed that far back is unknown.
- R2: no other gauge revised.
- R4: transition of about 38 to 40 hours with a phantom rise; afterwards the raw estimate about 25 to 30
  points higher against Little Falls than before; S-old shows errors like those of 11 to 13 Nov throughout.
- W1: 24-hour period; present on every day since at least 10 Nov; weaker on 15 and 16 Nov.
- W3: the wave reaches Little Falls sooner than the model's assumed travel time for the water; size at Little
  Falls about +/-5% of flow against about +/-13% at Point of Rocks.
- W4: the raw estimate is nearly flat over the day (the time-shifted sum and the rise/fall factor largely
  cancel) while Little Falls swings, giving a daily error cycle of about +/-6 points after the revision.
- W5: no firm expectation between H1 and H4; H5 cannot be excluded on 20 checks.
- W6: the checks fall at two times of day about 12 hours apart.

## 7. What would overturn the first pass (looked for actively)

- Old and current Point of Rocks flows agree within 2% at identical timestamps in the overlap windows: then
  there was no revision of that size, and the job log needs another explanation.
- Little Falls, or another input, changed as well (R2): then the "truth" moved, not only the input.
- The raw estimate under S-old is as wrong as the logged one after 14 Nov: then the revision is not the cause.
- The wave's size or timing changed on about 13 or 14 Nov by coincidence (H2): then part of the break belongs
  to the river, not to USGS.
- Withdrawals between Point of Rocks and Little Falls changed: not measurable with these data; looked for as
  an unexplained change in (Point of Rocks + tributaries - Little Falls) on the level record, and reported as
  a limit.

## 8. Verification

1. Blind dual-language analysis: a Python analyst and an R analyst, separately, from a written
   `ANALYSIS_SPEC.md` that the orchestrator writes after the tool audit and before any result exists. Neither
   sees the other's code or output. Every reported number appears in `metrics_python.csv` and `metrics_R.csv`
   and must agree within 0.01 (absolute, in the unit reported) or the study stops and investigates.
2. Third auditor (Opus), never one who ran an analysis: cross-language agreement, method against plan and
   spec, data integrity, the replay's fidelity table, and at least 10 spot checks of frozen values against the
   live USGS service (including at least 3 Point of Rocks flows, which must match the frozen current record,
   and at least 3 field measurements).
3. The first-pass numbers of section 1 are not inputs. The auditor reports which of them the study reproduced.

## 9. Roles, sequence, size

Fable orchestrates, rules on audits, writes the spec and the owner's report. Sonnet builds (data, replay tool)
and runs the two blind analyses. Opus audits the plan, the tools and the results. `model` is set on every
agent call. Builders do not commit.

1. Plan audit (Opus) -> rulings -> owner confirms (this document, section 13).
2. Freeze and tidy tables (Sonnet); replay tool (Sonnet).
3. Tool audit (Opus) -> fixes.
4. `ANALYSIS_SPEC.md` -> Python and R analysts (Sonnet, blind, parallel) -> comparison.
5. Third auditor (Opus).
6. Report to the owner: plain words, charts, every number tied to a file; then the remedies discussion.

About nine to eleven agent runs in total. The quality signals watched for the Sonnet tier: the fidelity gate,
cross-language agreement, and the third auditor's refutation count.

## 10. Out of scope

Remedies of any kind; any change to the app, the hourly job, the learned corrections or the database; any new
on-screen wording; the 48-hour forecast; the 90% band (TODO D21/A8). An earlier study of checking several
estimates at once exists (`analysis/multipending-responsiveness/`, 2026-11-11); it is not re-opened here.

## 11. Known limits

- Old Point of Rocks flows exist only where someone happened to save them: 5 to 22 Oct, 11 Oct, and 10 to 13
  Nov. The revision cannot be measured outside those windows.
- The detailed job log starts on 2026-11-10 21:00 UTC: 71 runs before the revision, about 200 after.
- One revision, one low-water period. The findings describe November 2026; R7 and W7 say how typical it is,
  with data of lower detail.
- Counterfactuals replay the raw estimate, not the learning.
- Withdrawals and returns between the gauges are not observed.
