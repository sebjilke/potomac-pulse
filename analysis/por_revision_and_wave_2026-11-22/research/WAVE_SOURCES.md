# Sources on a daily rise and fall at Point of Rocks at low flow

Compiled 2026-11-22. Labels: (a) stated by source, (b) my inference, (c) not found. No analysis of river data.

## 1. The documented explanation (a)

Three agencies or documents state a cause. All say: upstream hydroelectric dams shut down at night during the American eel migration, mid-September to mid-December, and this produces a daily pattern of flow downstream.

1. ICPRB, "News From Around the Basin", 24 Oct 2024 (https://potomacriver.org/?p=17371; raw copy data/wave_pages/icprb_2024-10-24.html, retrieved 2026-11-22 11:05 UTC): "From September to December, upstream hydroelectric dams turn off operations at night during the annual American eel migration downstream." and "As a result, the flows downstream fluctuate in this predictable daily pattern." It names the "USGS Point of Rocks gage" in the same item.
2. ICPRB, 23 Oct 2025 (https://www.potomacriver.org/news/news-from-around-the-basin-october-23-2025/): "Notice something interesting about the flow at the Point of Rocks gage? The daily fluctuations are a result of upstream hydroelectric dam operations to protect American eels. The dams are turned off at night during the annual American eel migration downstream (September to December)."
3. ICPRB, 11 Dec 2025 (https://www.potomacriver.org/news/news-from-around-the-basin-december-11-2025/): "We'll soon see the daily fluctuations flatten out at the USGS Point of Rocks gage." and "The dams are turned off nightly from mid-September to mid-December".
4. ICPRB CO-OP daily reports, autumn 2026 (e.g. https://icprbcoop.org/node/846, node/810, node/836; raw copies in data/coop/): "Intra-daily flow fluctuations associated with upstream dam operations for eel passage may continue to affect observed flows at Point of Rocks and other Potomac River gages." The 5 Oct 2026 report says "Intra-daily flow fluctuations at Potomac River gages, including Point of Rocks, are occurring due to upstream dam operations associated with eel passage." Count: 35 of the 37 fetched reports dated 1 Sep 2026 or later contain the word "eel" (first occurrence: the report dated 2026-09-15, node 820). The Aug 2026 reports (3 to 9 Aug) do not contain it. Which two Sep to Nov pages lack it was not checked.

Limit (c): none of these statements names which dams. The ICPRB items say "upstream hydroelectric dams" and give no operating hours, no size of the effect, and no measurements. I found no FERC document, USGS remark or agency report that quantifies the daily swing at Point of Rocks.

## 2. Best-documented single mechanism: Millville (Shenandoah) (a)

FERC draft environmental assessment for the Millville Hydroelectric Project, P-2343-086 (https://www.ferc.gov/sites/default/files/2020-06/P-2343EA.pdf; copy data/pdf/FERC_P-2343_Millville_EA_2020.pdf; retrieved 2026-11-22 ~11:07 UTC; file name suggests June 2020, not confirmed). Quotes:

- "As currently licensed, PE Hydro operates the project in run-of-river mode, such that outflow from the project approximates inflow. PE Hydro also provides at all times, a 200-cfs minimum flow ... The project shuts down nightly from September 15 to December 15 to facilitate downstream eel passage."
- "The project has no peaking capability".
- "Although the Millville Project operates in run-of-river mode, where inflow is equal to outflow, unique characteristics of the project's location and design contribute to fluctuations in flow when the project shuts down nightly from September 15 through December 15 ... When shutdowns occur, the Millville stream gauge shows a temporary decrease in flow (at approximately 6 p.m.). When the project resumes operations at 6 a.m., the Millville stream gauge shows a temporary increase in flow. The changes in flows at start-up and shut-down attenuate within approximately two hours (see figure 3)."
- Licensee statement: "the change in flows following unit shut-down or start-up attenuates within approximately two hours of a change in flow. The changes in flow result from several factors, including the shift in location of flow releases between the spillway and powerhouse, location and design of the power canal, and location of the stream gauge relative to the powerhouse tailrace."
- Plant: "3 turbine-generating units with a combined capacity of 2.84 megawatts"; located near Harpers Ferry, Jefferson County, WV; gauge "approximately 0.5 miles downstream of the project" (USGS 01636500).
- Paddling interest: commenters (River Riders, American Whitewater, Canoe Cruisers Association of Greater Washington, Potomac and Shenandoah Riverkeeper) "identify fluctuations and flow during eel passage project shutdowns as an area of interest for paddlers".

What this does and does not show: (a) a documented nightly shutdown schedule, 6 p.m. to 6 a.m. in the Millville case, with a flow change visible at the Millville gauge that dies out in about two hours. (b) The EA describes a change at the Millville gauge that is a local effect of spill versus powerhouse release and the gauge location; it does not say whether, or how much, a daily cycle reaches Point of Rocks, about 15 to 20 river miles downstream (my estimate from coordinates; not sourced). The licensee's "two hours" is for Millville only. The EA is a draft; the license was issued in Dec 2017 per a hydroreform.org listing (search-tool summary, not read), which conflicts with a 2020 date on the file name. Treat the date as unresolved.

Also: USGS Transactions of the American Fisheries Society paper, Eyler et al. 2016 (https://www.usgs.gov/publications/downstream-passage-and-impact-turbine-shutdowns-survival-silver-american-eels-five; raw copy data/wave_pages/usgs_eel_pub.html): "five run-of-the-river hydroelectric dams located over a 195-km stretch of the Shenandoah River, Virginia-West Virginia" and "nighttime turbine shutdowns (1800-0600 hours) were implemented during September 15-December 15." This is about the Shenandoah dams (study 2007 to 2010). It confirms the 1800 to 0600 schedule for the Shenandoah dams; it is not about the Potomac main stem.

## 3. Potomac main-stem dams 4 and 5 (a / c)

- Federal Register notice 2026-03575, scheduled for publication 23 Feb 2026 (data/pdf/FR_2026-03575_dam4_dam5_variance.pdf; https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-03575.pdf): "Dam No. 4 Hydro Station (P-2516)" and "Dam No. 5 Hydro Station (P-2517)", licensee PE Hydro Generation, LLC (c/o Eagle Creek Renewable Energy, Bethesda), "located on the Potomac River in Berkeley (P-2516 and P-2517) and Jefferson (P-2516) counties, West Virginia". The request: a temporary variance from the veil-flow requirement (Article 406) "for approximately 15 days at each dam ... between May 15, and September 15, 2026" for National Park Service inspections, "including reservoir drawdown and refill". Comment deadline 20 March 2026. Whether FERC granted it and whether it happened: not found. The window ends before the autumn eel season.
- Eagle Creek Renewable Energy (https://www.eaglecreekre.com/facilities/operating-facilities/potomac-and-shenandoah-river-facilities, retrieved 2026-11-22): "PE Hydro is a portfolio of seven run-of-river operating hydroelectric assets with a total capacity of 10.6 MW" "located in West Virginia and Virginia on the Potomac and Shenandoah Rivers." The page's menu lists Dam 4, Dam 5 and Millville; it names "The Warren, Luray, Newport, and Shenandoah facilities" in a relicensing paragraph and says "The facilities' current licenses expire December 31, 2023" (that sentence is for those four; the page is out of date for 2026).
- Capacity (search-tool summary of hydroreform.org / Wikipedia, not read in full): Dam 4 about 1.9 MW (3 units), Dam 5 about 1.0 MW (2 units); license terms 7 May 2004 to 26 Apr 2034; Dam 5 listed "run-of-river" (https://hydroreform.org/hydro-project/dam-5-p-2517/). Dam 4 mode: not found.
- (c) I found no FERC order, license article or agency document that says Dam 4 or Dam 5 shut down nightly for eels. ICPRB's wording "upstream hydroelectric dams" and CO-OP's "upstream dam operations for eel passage" do not name them. Eelways: FWS/ICPRB material mentions an eelway at Dam 5 and one planned at Dam 4 (search summaries; status not confirmed).

## 5. North Branch reservoirs and large daily-rhythm users

- Jennings Randolph and Savage: owned and operated by the US Army Corps of Engineers (ICPRB Water Supply Outlook text, via search summary of the July 2026 edition). CO-OP daily reports point readers to "observed releases from Savage and Jennings Randolph reservoirs" at https://water.usace.army.mil/office/nab/ and "scheduled releases" at https://water.usace.army.mil/office/nab/releases/north-branch (quoted from node/846). I did not retrieve those pages (not needed for withdrawals; they are the place to check releases for Sep to Nov 2026). Releases: not found for autumn 2026.
- ICPRB Sept 2025 item (search summary, not read): artificially varied flow release from Jennings Randolph planned 20 to 21 Sep 2025 and a Savage whitewater release the next week. This is 2025, and such releases are one-off events, not a daily rhythm (b).
- Large industrial or municipal users with a daily rhythm: none found. Water supply draws are all downstream of Point of Rocks (section 5 of the other file), so they cannot cause a wave at Point of Rocks (b).

## 7. Other facts that may matter for W2

- Timing of the season: ICPRB says "September to December" and "mid-September to mid-December" (a). Millville's schedule is 15 Sep to 15 Dec (a). The CO-OP "Special Conditions" sentence appears in the autumn reports from mid-September (a: first fetched report with the sentence is dated 2026-09-15; no reports exist for 1 to 13 Sep, so the true first date is unknown).
- The ICPRB 11 Dec 2025 item implies the pattern flattens when the shutdown season ends (a: "We'll soon see the daily fluctuations flatten out").
- Clocks: Millville times are given in local clock time ("approximately 6 p.m.", "6 a.m."); the EA does not say standard or daylight time (c).
- Nothing in the sources links the wave to a weekday or weekend pattern (c).
