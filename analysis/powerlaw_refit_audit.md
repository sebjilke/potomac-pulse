## 2. Autocorrelation Analysis

### Finding: Extreme autocorrelation confirmed

The hourly OLS residuals exhibit near-unit-root autocorrelation:
- **Default model**: DW=0.007, ACF(1)=0.997, ACF(24)=0.881, ACF(168)=0.793
- **Cold model**: DW=0.065, ACF(1)=0.968, ACF(24)=0.451, ACF(168)=0.051

All Ljung-Box tests reject the null of no autocorrelation at every lag tested (p ≈ 0).

### Impact on estimation

**Point estimates are UNBIASED**: OLS point estimates are unbiased even with autocorrelated errors — this is a standard econometric result (Gauss-Markov theorem). Autocorrelation inflates precision (narrows CIs) but does not bias slope or intercept estimates.

**Verification via subsampling**: The 24h and 168h subsamples (which break the hourly autocorrelation) produce virtually identical point estimates:

| Model | Method | coef | exp | Δcoef vs OLS | Δexp vs OLS |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Default | OLS | 127.83 | 2.4536 | — | — |
| Default | Sub-24h | 127.95 | 2.4527 | +0.1% | -0.0009 |
| Default | Sub-168h | 132.09 | 2.4315 | +3.3% | -0.0221 |
| Cold | OLS | 166.36 | 2.3414 | — | — |
| Cold | Sub-24h | 167.00 | 2.3417 | +0.4% | +0.0003 |
| Cold | Sub-168h | 153.35 | 2.3938 | -7.8% | +0.0524 |

The 24h subsamples (n≈4,900 for default, n=540 for cold) are essentially identical to OLS. The 168h subsamples (n=701 default, n=78 cold) show slightly more variation due to smaller sample size, but still within a few percent.

**Newey-West HAC correctly widens CIs**: The HAC standard errors produce wider confidence intervals that account for the autocorrelation. The v29.0 parameter values (126 and 2.46) fall within these wider CIs for both Python and R implementations.

**GLS Cochrane-Orcutt is inappropriate**: With rho ≈ 0.997, this method is numerically unstable. This is expected — Cochrane-Orcutt assumes stationary AR(1), but near-unit-root processes violate this assumption. This method should be DISCARDED for this dataset.

### Conclusion on autocorrelation

1. Hourly residuals are heavily autocorrelated (expected for hourly hydrological data)
2. Point estimates are unbiased (confirmed by subsampling)
3. Standard OLS CIs are artificially narrow (corrected by Newey-West HAC)
4. GLS Cochrane-Orcutt fails due to near-unit-root process (discard)
5. **No correction or alternative model is needed** — the autocorrelation does not bias the power-law estimates

The plan specified: "If coefficients change materially (e.g., >5% in coef or >0.05 in exponent) across BOTH autocorrelation-corrected methods, we update the model."

- Default model: +1.4% coef, -0.006 exp → **NOT material** ✅
- Cold model: +4.0% coef, -0.019 exp → Below 5% threshold, below 0.05 exp threshold → **NOT material** ✅
- Subsampling confirms OLS estimates are stable → OLS is not biased by autocorrelation ✅

**Recommendation**: Do NOT update the power-law parameters. v29.0 values (126×EF^2.46, 160×EF^2.36) are validated on the 117k hourly dataset.
