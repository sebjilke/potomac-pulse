## Findings

### F1. BLOCKER. Section 5, state rule: half the low-water hours and 42% of all test hours get a clock that was not measured for them
Evidence (replay arm L, test hours 2020 on, the plan's rule applied to `lfCap` and the row 3 h earlier): 60,120 hours; rising 10,444, falling 15,456, low water 8,876, "otherwise the rising relation" 25,344 (42%). Of the 18,161 test hours with Little Falls below 3,000 cfs, only 8,876 are "low water"; 3,959 are "rising" and 5,326 "falling", because the daily wave moves Little Falls by 2% in 3 hours. The rising relation is fitted on rises whose window median is almost never below 3,000 cfs (5 units in all years, section on counts below). So at eel-season flows the clock would flip hour by hour between the low-water value (about 6 h by the plan's own expectation) and an extrapolated rising value, and the large steady group above 3,000 cfs gets the shortest clock of all.
Change. Replace the state rule with: "Low water: the Little Falls reading is below 3,000 cfs and the hour is not inside a rise window (section 4.2, extended as in F11). Rising: inside a rise window, from its start to the Little Falls peak. Falling: inside a recession window. Otherwise (steady, 3,000 cfs and above): the relation fitted on all rise and recession units together." Report the 2% / 3 h rule as a sensitivity only.

### F2. BLOCKER. Section 4.7, the verdict is probably MIXED by construction, and it ignores low water
Evidence: rise units from 2020 on, by class of window median Little Falls flow, before any gate (window from 6 h before onset or before `rl_start`, the plan does not say which): below 3,000: 3; 3,000 to 6,000: 13 to 21 (mostly small onsets); 6,000 to 12,000: 20 to 28; 12,000 to 25,000: 6 to 8; 25,000 and above: 0 to 1. After the 0.6 gate, the 24 paired hours, the end-of-range rule and the creek-heavy cut, the 12,000 to 25,000 class can fall below 5 and the 3,000 to 6,000 class is mostly onsets that last 0 to 6 h (F11). With fewer than three classes holding five units, neither TOO LONG nor ABOUT RIGHT can occur and the verdict is MIXED whatever the river does. The largest gap between the app and every earlier look is at low water (app 13.3 h at 1,500 cfs), which the verdict does not judge. The second TOO LONG condition is redundant when the range is the default ×/÷1.33 (1.5 times the centre already lies above it).
Change. Replace 4.7 by: "Three flow classes (below 6,000; 6,000 to 12,000; 12,000 and above) and two states (rising; low water), all years pooled, interval by bootstrap over events. Per cell with at least 5 units: TOO LONG if the app's value is above the long end of the measured range; TOO SHORT if below the short end; inside otherwise. Verdict TOO LONG if at least two cells are TOO LONG and none TOO SHORT; ABOUT RIGHT if every qualifying cell is inside; NOT DECIDED if fewer than two cells qualify; MIXED otherwise." Compare against both the formula (with its 1,000 cfs floor) and the replay's own `travelGFtoLF` on the same hours (it carries the rise reduction and the non-converged flow, which the formula alone does not).

### F3. MAJOR. Section 4.2/4.3, recession lags are not identifiable by correlation of changes
Evidence (reasoning, no data): on a recession close to exponential, the hourly change is itself an exponential, and a shifted exponential is the same curve times a constant. The correlation is then near 1 at every shift; its maximum is set by noise, the daily wave, withdrawals or the window edge. The window starts 3 h after the Little Falls peak, so the crest, the one feature that fixes the shift, is cut out. Expect many maxima at the ends of the range (excluded) and a falling clock that reflects the window, not the river.
Change. "Recession window: from 6 h before the Little Falls peak to 72 h after it." Add: "If fewer than 60% of recession units pass, or the median falling lag differs from the median rising lag of the same units by more than half the rising lag, the falling relation is not used; falling hours take the all-units relation (F1)."

### F4. MAJOR. Section 4.6, the test of the split cannot detect a faster gorge, and the range is too narrow on the short side
Evidence (arithmetic): let Point of Rocks to Great Falls move at speed v and Great Falls to Little Falls at k × v. The speed ratio of the lower leg to the upper leg is then 1.13 (k = 1.5), 1.21 (k = 2), 1.31 (k = 3): "supported" in every case. The split by distance then overstates the true time by 1.32, 1.65 and 2.3 times. The gorge (the falls, Mather Gorge, the pool above Little Falls dam) is hydraulically unlike both gauged legs; equal speed on the two legs says nothing about it. The plan's section 8 already says the central value is more likely too long; the range ×/÷1.33 is symmetric anyway.
Change. Drop "supported / not supported" as a test of the split; report the speed ratio as description. Replace the range by: "long end = distance share of the measured lower-leg lag (no gorge slower than the pooled reach); central = distance share; short end = 0.4 × distance share, unless the direct check (F5) is valid, in which case the short end is the lower of 0.75 × distance share and the direct check's lower quartile." Say in the report that the split is an assumption, bounded, not tested.

### F5. MAJOR. Section 4.6, the Difficult Run selection leaves too few pulses and looks at the wrong gauge window
Evidence (Seneca Creek as a stand-in, the only creek in the file): 623 separate peaks (rise of 50 cfs or more over 24 h, 48 h apart); with Little Falls below 6,000 cfs the day before and Point of Rocks within 5% over the 48 h around the peak, 7 remain (33 at 10%, 99 at 20%). Point of Rocks varies by 5% on the daily wave alone. The window is also wrong: water at Little Falls in the 24 h after the pulse passed Point of Rocks one to three days earlier, and Edwards Ferry 8 to 17 h earlier. The same storm raises Cabin John Creek (enters about 2 miles above the gauge), Seneca Creek and Broad Run; their water reaches Little Falls at different times and pulls the lag both ways. Positives: the Difficult Run gauge (38.9759, -77.2458) sits near Georgetown Pike, by coordinates well under a mile above the mouth, so the creek's own travel is small.
Change. "Units: separate Difficult Run peaks with a rise of at least 10% of the Little Falls flow of the day before, that flow below 6,000 cfs; Edwards Ferry stage range below 5% of its value over [peak - 24 h, peak + 12 h]; Seneca and Broad Run rises over the same window each below 25% of the Difficult Run rise in cfs. Take all that pass, up to 40, largest first. If fewer than 10 pass, the check is reported as description only and does not set the range." With Seneca as the stand-in this rule leaves 96 peaks at the stage condition and 27 with a pulse of 10% or more. Distance: Difficult Run mouth to the Little Falls gauge is about 7.2 to 7.6 miles, not 6.9; scale to 8.9 to 9.4 (F13). Seneca and Broad Run pulses are not a better direct check: both enter above Great Falls, and their gauges sit about 5 and 2.5 creek miles above the mouths, an unknown lag that swamps a 2 to 3 h answer. Report them as description only, if at all.

### F6. MAJOR. Section 4.2/4.3, low-water weeks: the method cannot tell L from L + 24 h
Evidence: subtracting the 25-h mean makes the series closer to a pure daily cycle; its correlation at L and L + 24 differs only by edge effects and day-to-day amplitude. The ambiguity rule (second maximum within 0.05) will flag nearly every week over 0 to 72 h, or keep a few weeks chosen because something non-periodic happened. Either way the survivors are not a fair sample. A second source of bias: Fairfax Water, WSSC and both Washington Aqueduct intakes withdraw between Edwards Ferry and Little Falls, a large share of the flow at 1,500 cfs, with a daily pattern tied to clock time, which shifts the phase at Little Falls. Counts: 149 low-water weeks pass the plan's rules (pre 2020: 62; from 2020: 87; 72 start in the eel season).
Change. "Low-water weeks give the phase only (lag modulo 24 h, circular median and spread per class). The whole-day count comes from low-flow bumps: Little Falls from a base below 3,000 cfs rising by 40% or more, peak below 6,000, measured as rises (section 4.3, 0 to 72 h). The low-water lag is the phase plus the whole days that put it nearest the bump median; also check that Point of Rocks to Edwards Ferry plus Edwards Ferry to Little Falls equals Point of Rocks to Little Falls within 3 h." Counts: 123 such bumps, 113 with Edwards Ferry complete over the 48 h before (51 before 2020). Many will be creek-led; the creek rule (F8) applies. State the withdrawal bias as a limit. Say whether the low-water XC runs on detrended levels or on changes (the plan does not).

### F7. MAJOR. Section 4.1, the log of a stage biases rises and recessions in opposite directions
Evidence: with a rating Q = a(h - h0)^b, d ln Q = b × h/(h - h0) × d ln h. The factor falls as stage rises (h0 > 0), so ln h underweights the early, low-stage part of a rise at Edwards Ferry relative to Little Falls flow. The Edwards Ferry pulse looks later, so the lag looks shorter on rises and longer on recessions. Edwards Ferry stage runs from about 3.0 ft at low water to 10 ft (95th percentile), so the factor can change by a large amount within one rise. Direction on rises matches the expected verdict. Stage resolution is 0.01 ft (0.3% of 3 ft), also noisy at low water.
Change. "Primary for the two pairs with Edwards Ferry: Edwards Ferry stage against Little Falls stage (`lf_stage`), both raw hourly differences. Sensitivity: the plan's log version, and raw stage against raw flow. If the median lag of a class differs by more than 1 h between versions, the report says so and the clock range takes both."

### F8. MAJOR. Section 4.4, the creek-heavy rule looks at the wrong hour and the wrong quantity
Evidence: the creek gauges sit far above their mouths (by USGS coordinates: Goose Creek about 10 creek miles, Monocacy at Jug Bridge over 20, Seneca at Dawsonville about 5, Broad Run about 2.5). At the hour of the Little Falls peak, creek water that made the early rise is often already receding at those gauges. The rule compares creek flow (not its rise) with Little Falls' rise. Gauged creeks are 177 of the 430 square miles between Edwards Ferry and Little Falls; Difficult Run, Cabin John, Watts Branch, Muddy Branch, Sugarland Run and others are not counted. Local water reaches Little Falls first and pulls the lower-leg lag short.
Change. "Creek share of a unit = (sum over the window of each gauged creek's flow above its value at the window start) × (local drainage / gauged drainage), divided by the sum over the window of Little Falls' flow above its value at the window start. Lower leg: Seneca, Broad Run and Difficult Run (once fetched), local area 430 square miles. Upper leg: Monocacy and Goose, local area 1,479. Creek-heavy if the share exceeds 15%. Report lags with and without the cut."

### F9. MAJOR. Section 4.3, one number for three different travel times
Evidence: the best-lining-up shift of changes measures the timing of the steepest part. Diffusion makes a start of rise arrive earlier and an attenuating crest later. STEEP is the same feature again. The plan cannot answer its own question 2 (start, steepest part, crest).
Change. Add, per rise unit and pair: "times at which each series first reaches 10%, 50% and 90% of its rise in the window (linear between hours), and the times of the two crests; lag = downstream minus upstream for each." The clock stays XC; the report states how far the 10% and 90% lags sit from it.

### F10. MAJOR. Section 5, the yardstick and cap readings lean with the length of the clock
Evidence: the app's estimate does not depend on the clock; Y0 and Y1 are built from Little Falls itself. A shorter clock puts the truth nearer to "Little Falls now", which favours Y0 and Y1 and the cap (it lowers values) on rising hours, by construction of the comparison, not by skill. Reading 1 is nearly one-directional: on a rising limb Little Falls only grows, P sits below the truth on most such hours (the cap study's 43% badly low), so any shorter clock lowers P's error. The plan's own expectations (Y1 beats the app on rising hours, the cap does a good job everywhere) are what the shorter clock gives for free. Y1 is not fully defined ("carried forward": linear in cfs or in log, over T or T plus the reading's age).
Change. "A yardstick beats the app, or the cap does a good job or harm, only if the reading is the same under the central clock and both ends of the range; otherwise UNCLEAR." Reading 1 becomes description: signed and absolute error of P under both clocks, and the hours that cross the 15% line in each direction. Define Y1 as `LF_now + (LF_now - LF_(t-3)) / 3 × (T + age_lf_h)`, floored at `LF_now`. Split large-rise hours into Point of Rocks led and Little Falls led, as the cap audit asked (F3 there).

### F11. MAJOR. Section 4.2, the small onsets are not rises, and the window start is undefined
Evidence: of the 33 small onsets (class BELOW), 15 have the peak at the onset hour and 27 peak within 6 h; the rise list has no `rl_start` for them. With the window anchored at `onset_t0`, every one of them spans 13 to 25 h, short of the 24 paired hours rule in most cases. "The start of the rising limb" is not a column in the file; for events, `rl_start` is `t0 - 12 h` by definition, not a start. Merged events carry several onsets; the plan does not say one unit or several.
Change. "Start of the rising limb = the last hour before `onset_t0` at which Little Falls is at its minimum over the 48 h before `onset_t0`. One unit per event (`event_t0`, `event_tp`). A small onset is a unit only if Little Falls rises by at least 30% from that start to its peak and the window holds at least 24 paired hours." Add the low-flow bumps of F6 as rising units for the classes below 6,000 cfs.

### F12. MAJOR. Section 4.5, the pre-2020 fit protects nothing that matters and halves the units
Evidence: the clock is fitted on gauge lags, never on the app's errors, so section 5 cannot be flattered through it. The split only checks the clock's own stability, at the price of half the units (pre-2020 rising: about 36 to 39 units, several classes with fewer than 5). Units overlap (merged events, small onsets near events, recessions cut by the next unit), so a bootstrap over units overstates precision. Theil and Sen through units mostly between 3,000 and 12,000 cfs says little about 1,500 cfs.
Change. "The relation is fitted on all years; the two halves (before and from 2020) are fitted separately and reported as a stability check. Bootstrap over events (a unit belongs to the event whose window it overlaps most), 10,000 draws. The relation is not used outside the flow range of its units; below that range the low-water relation applies."

## Answers to the nine questions

1. **Section 2 against facts, distances and order.** Section 2 matches `FACTS.md` and the code (one line-number slip, `:1116` for `:1117`). Distances (C&O Canal towpath mileposts, NPS-derived lock table, plus USGS station coordinates): Point of Rocks gauge 48.2 (Lock 28 at 48.9 lies just above), Monocacy mouth 42.2 (Monocacy Aqueduct), Goose Creek mouth at about 31, on the Virginia bank some 0.5 km above the Edwards Ferry gauge (mouth 39.101, -77.477 against the gauge 39.0986, -77.4713), Edwards Ferry 30.9 (Lock 25), Broad Run (Virginia) mouth below Edwards Ferry at about mile 28 to 29 (mouth 39.071, -77.444, at the south end of Selden Island; the "Broad Run Trunk" at towpath mile 31.9 is a different, Maryland stream), Seneca Creek 22.7 (Seneca Aqueduct), Great Falls 14.3 to 14.4 (Tavern, Lock 20), Difficult Run about 12.5 (end of Mather Gorge, Virginia side; the gorge runs from the falls to Difficult Run Rapids), Cabin John Creek about 7.5 (mouth 38.970, -77.146, Glen Echo), Little Falls gauge about 5.0 to 5.4. So Monocacy and Goose lie above Edwards Ferry (as the drainage of 11,130 square miles implies), Broad Run and Seneca between Edwards Ferry and Great Falls, Difficult Run and Cabin John between Great Falls and the gauge. Confidence: lock mileposts high; creek mouths to within about half a mile (coordinates from secondary sources, matched by eye); towpath and river miles differ by a few percent at most on this reach. Legs: Point of Rocks to Edwards Ferry 17.3; Edwards Ferry to Little Falls 25.4 to 25.9; Great Falls to Little Falls 8.9 to 9.4 (share 0.35 to 0.37). The repository's "Edwards Ferry about 2 miles above Great Falls" is wrong; it is about 16.5.
2. **Is the XC lag the right measure?** For the steepest part of a rise, yes; for start and crest, no (F9). The log of a stage biases rises short and recessions long (F7). Hourly medians are neutral between gauges if both are placed at the hour's centre; they limit resolution, and the parabola pulls toward whole hours (F17). The window in downstream time with the upstream shifted is correct as such, but the recession window cuts out the crest, and recessions are not identifiable by this measure (F3). The 0.6 gate keeps clean, larger, faster rises (F14). Bias rising against falling: yes, from F3 and F7, in opposite directions.
3. **The split and the direct check.** The split by distance is an assumption; the 0.75 to 1.33 reading cannot detect a gorge up to three times faster (F4). Difficult Run can work, but not with the selection as written (7 units at most, judged from Seneca); use the Edwards Ferry window and creek rules of F5. Seneca and Broad Run pulses are not a better direct check (they enter above Great Falls and their gauges are miles above their mouths).
4. **Low-water 24 h ambiguity.** The daily wave alone cannot separate L from L + 24. Setting ambiguous weeks aside keeps a selected few. Use phase from the weeks and the day count from low-flow bumps and the chain (F6).
5. **Pre-2020 fit as protection.** Not a real protection for the scoring: the clock is fitted on gauge lags, not on the app's errors. Fit all years, report the halves (F12).
6. **Section 5.** The truth "Little Falls at the hour of the estimate plus the clock" is right if the hour is `predTs` and the hourly medians are placed at their centres (F15). The state rule is not sound (F1). The yardsticks see the same reading as the app, which is fair, but their readings and the cap reading lean with the clock's length; require the same reading at all three clocks (F10). Reading 1 is nearly one-directional (F10).
7. **Section 4.7.** The three outcomes are mutually exclusive, but with the counts in hand TOO LONG and ABOUT RIGHT are probably out of reach and MIXED absorbs "too few units". The second TOO LONG condition is redundant at the default range. Replace as in F2.
8. **What is missing.** The cap study lesson on one-directional measures (F10, reading 1, and the direction of the clock in every section 5 reading); the Point of Rocks led / Little Falls led split from the cap audit; a "not decided" outcome; the low-water state in the verdict; level crossings (F9); a definition of the limb start (F11); bootstrap over events (F12); withdrawals at low water (F6); the 26 and 27 November rise (F16).
9. **The case against running it.** See below.

## The strongest case against running it

The clock does not touch the number the app shows. Every earlier look, on three different pairs and with two methods, puts the app's Great Falls to Little Falls time at two to four times what the gauged legs imply below about 12,000 cfs (app 6.5 h at 5,000 cfs and 13.3 h at 1,500 cfs, against about 3 h and about 6 h by distance from the measured Edwards Ferry lags). No refinement in section 4 can close a factor of two, and the one uncertainty that matters most, the speed through the gorge, stays an assumption whatever is run (F4). Section 5 then judges an app whose corrections were learned on the old clock; the plan itself says what a new clock does to the learning needs a new replay in the build plan, which will redo section 5 on better terms. A smaller study (section 4 for the Edwards Ferry to Little Falls leg on rises and low water, with F1 to F12 applied, plus Difficult Run) and then the build plan would answer the same decision.

Against that: the build plan needs a measured clock with a range, by flow and state, from two blind analysts; the first look is one script, one pair, integer lags, no gate on creeks, and its low-water figure inherits the 24 h ambiguity. Section 4 is worth running. Section 5 as a verdict is not, until F1 and F10 are applied.

Verdict: PROCEED AFTER CHANGES. F1 and F2 must be fixed before a specification is written; F3 to F12 should be.
