There is no gauge at Great Falls. Potomac Pulse estimates what is happening there right now and what is coming, so you can plan your time on the water.

1. The Nowcast

What is happening at Great Falls right now

Water takes roughly a day to flow from Point of Rocks to Great Falls. To estimate conditions at Great Falls right now, we look at what Point of Rocks was reading roughly 5 to 50 hours ago (depending on how fast the river is moving — about 19 hours at typical flows, much longer in low water), then adjust for everything that happens along the way.

Time-shifting
The primary input is the Point of Rocks gauge, ~34 river miles upstream. Travel time depends on flow: roughly 5 hours at very high water and about 19 hours at typical flows, lengthening to ~50 hours in low water. We look back by that amount to find the reading that corresponds to Great Falls right now. If the river is currently rising or falling at Point of Rocks, the estimate adjusts for how much of that change has reached Great Falls.
Tributaries and blending
Four tributaries enter the Potomac between Point of Rocks and Great Falls. Their current flows are added to the time-shifted estimate. The result is then blended with an independent estimate from the Edwards Ferry gauge, ~16 river miles above the falls. Edwards Ferry measures water level only, so a calibrated power-law model converts its stage reading to flow. At low flows the blend gives Edwards Ferry almost no weight; at high flows it contributes up to 40%.
Self-correcting
Every hour, the server checks its earlier prediction against what Little Falls actually recorded when that water arrived. The error is stored by flow level and flow direction (rising, falling, or steady), and a correction factor is updated so the next prediction in similar conditions is closer. The model improves continuously, even when no one is looking at the site. The estimate is also capped at 120% of the downstream gauge reading to prevent runaway overestimates during fast rises.

2. The 48-Hour Forecast

What is coming to Great Falls

The forecast uses a different method from the nowcast. The National Weather Service predicts what will arrive at Little Falls, the downstream gauge. Since water passes Great Falls before it reaches Little Falls, we shift the NWS prediction earlier in time by the travel time between the two gauges (roughly 6 hours at typical flows). If NWS says Little Falls will rise at 6 PM, Great Falls should rise around noon.

NWS forecasts can drift from actual gauge readings, so we apply an additive bias correction: the difference between what NWS currently predicts for Little Falls and what the gauge currently reads. This offset is recalculated every 15 minutes and automatically shrinks as NWS updates its models.
