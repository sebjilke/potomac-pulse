📊 How It Works

Weighted Ensemble: GF estimate = 60% (PoR model) + 40% (Edwards Ferry model). The PoR model uses time-shifted upstream data + tributaries; the EF model uses a power-law correlation (R²=0.98) from a gauge just 2-3 miles upstream of GF.

PoR Model: Point of Rocks flow (83.5%) is time-shifted by ~19 hrs (at median flow), then Monocacy (7.1%) and Goose Creek (3.0%) are added.

EF Model: LF_cfs = 108 × EF_stage^2.64 — built from 17,000 paired observations with time-shift optimization and hysteresis analysis.

Validation: Every estimate gets checked ~6-7 hrs later when water reaches Little Falls. The system learns flow-binned corrections (0-3k, 3-6k, 6-12k, etc.) for rising/falling/steady conditions.

⚠️ Limitations

This is an estimate, not a measurement. Accuracy depends on:
• Monocacy + Goose Creek contributions (~10% of flow)
• Travel time model accuracy at current flow
• Accumulated learning observations
• Absence of localized storm events between PoR and GF
• Water withdrawals (DC Aqueduct, WSSC, Fairfax Water) - can reduce flow 5-15% at low flows; learning system corrects for this over time

What This App Does

This app tracks water levels across the Potomac River basin and predicts when water from upstream will arrive at downstream locations. It's designed for paddlers who want to know conditions at Great Falls and Little Falls.

How Travel Times Work

Water takes time to flow downstream. The speed depends on how much water is in the river:

ConditionPoint of Rocks to Little FallsRiver Speed
Low flow~35 hours~1.2 mph
Typical~26 hours~1.6 mph
High flow~14 hours~3 mph
Flood~7 hours~6 mph

Based on USGS Circular 438 (Searcy, 1961) with empirical correction (×0.80).

Great Falls Estimation

There's no gauge at Great Falls, so we estimate it using a weighted ensemble of two models:

GF = 60% × (PoR model) + 40% × (Edwards Ferry model)

PoR Model (60%): Time-shifted Point of Rocks reading from ~19 hours ago at median flow (faster at high flows ~11 hrs, slower at low flows ~26 hrs), plus Monocacy (7.1%) and Goose Creek (3.0%) tributaries.

Edwards Ferry Model (40%): Power-law correlation from 17,000 paired observations (R²=0.98). Edwards Ferry is just 2-3 miles upstream of Great Falls.
LF_cfs = 108 × EF_stage^2.64

Why ensemble? PoR is 20+ miles upstream but has discharge data; EF is much closer but only has stage. Blending both captures distant upstream changes AND local conditions near GF.

Validation: Every estimate gets checked ~6-7 hours later (at median flow) when that water reaches the Little Falls gauge. The system learns flow-binned correction factors for different conditions.

Accuracy: Continuously validated against actual LF readings. Current accuracy shown in the main display (e.g., "97.2% based on 169 validations").

Uncertainty: With 5+ observations per flow bin, shows a range (e.g., "3.2 ft (3.0-3.4)") representing ±1 standard deviation.

Background learning: A scheduled job runs every 4 hours to fetch data, validate predictions, and update corrections—even when no browsers are open.

Data Sources

Real-time data: USGS Water Services (updates every 15 minutes)
Forecasts: National Weather Service river predictions
Travel time model: USGS Circular 438 (Searcy & Davis, 1961)

Accuracy & Limitations

Point of Rocks to Little Falls: ±10% at normal flows, ±17% at flood stage
Upstream gauges (Hancock, Cumberland): ±10-20% - useful for "pulse is coming" awareness, not precise timing
Great Falls estimate: Depends on learned corrections; accuracy improves over time
Rapid changes: Model assumes steady flow; fast-moving flood waves may arrive sooner than predicted
